Is the Summit Z right for a software company?

V2 drew the Summit Z for a consumer product. V2.1 makes Zirven a technology and software company that will own many products. Before keeping the mark, we tested it again against two alternatives that add product-system and layered meaning.

Criteria
  • 01Distinctive. Recognisable among other Z marks in technology. No look-alikes.
  • 02Meaningful. Progress, hard problems, the next level, product evolution.
  • 03Small sizes. Holds at 16, 24 and 32 px, in a tab, a dock and a header.
  • 04Monochrome. Works in one colour, embossed, etched or stitched.
  • 05Scales to a portfolio. Can sit above VEVO and future products without competing with them.
  • 06Simple. Can be drawn from memory.

The candidates

All three share the V2 geometry: a 20° ramp that climbs to a 35° summit, a 55° descent and a base bar of one module (m).

A · Summit Z, refinedSelected

The V2 mark, redrawn. One solid form: the climb, the summit and the base. The angles now relate to each other (20° + 35° = 55°), the stroke weights are balanced, and a separate micro master handles 16–24 px.

B · Summit, modularProduct system

The same silhouette cut into three modules: the ramp, the descent and the base. Reads as a system of parts that make one product. The cuts follow the ramp and the base.

C · Summit, layeredConnected layers

Two layers: the summit (ramp) resting on a platform (descent and base), separated by one gap. Reads as a product layer on a shared platform.

Decision

Keep the Summit Z: candidate A, refined.

The original idea is the strongest and the most usable mark. The alternatives were not wasted. Their ideas go into the system, where they help more than they would inside the symbol:

  • B's modules become motion. In animated intros the three modules travel along the 20° ramp and lock into the solid Z (shown on the left).
  • C's layers become architecture. Product diagrams show products sitting on a shared Zirven platform layer.
  • A gets a micro master. A thinner ramp and a 57° descent keep the counters open at 16 px, with the base snapped to whole pixels.
  • The wordmark becomes lowercase. In capitals, Turkish readers see ZIRVEN as "Zırven", because Turkish capitalises i as İ. Lowercase zirven reads correctly in every language and sits calmly beside the capital-Z symbol.

What changed in A

Angles

Ramp 20°, summit 35°, descent 55°. Two of the angles add up to the third, so the construction can be checked.

Weights

Ramp and base are one module each (10 units). The descent is 11.5 units measured perpendicular. That is optically even, so the diagonal no longer looks thin.

Micro master

Below 24 px a separate drawing is used, with a ramp of 8.4 and a descent of 57°. At 16 px the base is exactly 2 px.

Lockup

Symbol height is 1.62 × the wordmark's x-height, the gap is 0.30 × symbol height, and the symbol sits on the baseline.

Construction

Grid
64-unit artboard. Symbol bounding box 42 × 50 units.
Module (m)
10 units, the height of the base bar. Clear space is 2m on every side.
Ramp
Climbs at 20° from the left edge to the summit at the top right.
Summit
35° interior angle: sharp enough to read as a peak, wide enough to survive printing.
Descent
55° from the summit to the base. 20 + 35 = 55.
Source
scripts/build_brand.py, class Summit. Every symbol file is generated from these numbers.

Resemblance check

Other Z marks in technology

We compared the mark visually with split-geometry Z marks, stacked-bar Z marks common in crypto, lightning-bolt Zs and Z-in-a-circle app icons. A's asymmetric ramp, acute summit and flat base do not match any of these silhouettes. B came closest to the stacked-bar group, which is one reason it was rejected.

Before registration

This is a visual review, not legal clearance. Before filing, commission a trademark search for classes 9, 35 and 42 in the TÜRKPATENT, EUIPO, WIPO (Madrid) and USPTO registers, and check app-store icon similarity.
